What Happens When Your Nervous System Is Stressed?

When your brain perceives a threat or pressure, your body activates the sympathetic nervous system, often called the "fight-or-flight" response.

This stress response can increase:

  • Heart rate
  • Breathing rate
  • Blood pressure
  • Muscle tension
  • Stress hormones such as cortisol

According to the National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH), prolonged stress may contribute to sleep problems, anxiety, headaches, digestive issues, and other health concerns.

The body also has a built-in counterbalance called the parasympathetic nervous system, often referred to as the "rest-and-digest" system. One of its most important pathways is the vagus nerve.

The Vagus Nerve: Your Body's Relaxation Highway

The vagus nerve is a major communication pathway between the brain and many organs, including the heart and lungs.

Research suggests that activities that stimulate parasympathetic activity, including slow breathing exercises, can help reduce physiological arousal and promote relaxation.

While you can't directly control every aspect of the nervous system, breathing is one function that allows you to influence it intentionally. Slow, controlled breathing sends signals associated with safety and relaxation, helping the body shift away from a stress response.

Breathing Patterns to Calm Your Nervous System

1. Longer Exhales

One of the simplest ways to encourage relaxation is to make your exhale slightly longer than your inhale.

Try this:

  1. Inhale for 4 seconds.
  2. Exhale for 6 seconds.
  3. Repeat for 2 minutes.

Studies have found that breathing practices emphasizing longer exhalations may help reduce physiological arousal and improve mood.

2. Box Breathing

Box breathing is a structured technique commonly used to develop focus and emotional regulation.

Try this:

  1. Inhale for 4 seconds.
  2. Hold for 4 seconds.
  3. Exhale for 4 seconds.
  4. Hold for 4 seconds.
  5. Repeat for 2 minutes.

The predictable rhythm can help shift attention away from stressful thoughts and toward the present moment.

3. Coherent Breathing

Coherent breathing uses a slow and steady rhythm.

Try this:

  1. Inhale for 5 seconds.
  2. Exhale for 5 seconds.
  3. Continue for 2 minutes.

Researchers have found that slow, controlled breathing may support relaxation and help regulate physiological stress responses.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the vagus nerve?

The vagus nerve is a major communication pathway between the brain and many organs, including the heart and lungs. It's a key part of the parasympathetic nervous system, sometimes called the "rest-and-digest" system, and it plays a central role in helping the body shift out of a stress response and into a state of relaxation.

Can breathing exercises calm the nervous system?

Yes. Research suggests that slow, controlled breathing can help stimulate parasympathetic activity and reduce physiological arousal. Because breathing is one of the few automatic body functions you can consciously control, it offers a direct way to influence your nervous system in the moment.

How long does it take to feel relaxed from breathing exercises?

Many people notice a shift after just a couple of minutes of slow breathing. The techniques in this guide, like longer exhales, box breathing, and coherent breathing, are designed to be done in about two minutes, making them easy to fit into a busy day.

What breathing pattern is best for stress relief?

Patterns that emphasize a longer exhale than inhale, such as inhaling for 4 seconds and exhaling for 6, tend to be especially effective for stress relief, since a long exhale signals safety to the nervous system. Box breathing and coherent breathing are also strong options if you prefer a steady, structured rhythm.
